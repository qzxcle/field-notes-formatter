# Assembling an issue and writing the subject line

Read this when the editor asks for a full issue, the section order, a subject line or the preview text.

## Issue structure

The current format has been in use since July 2026.

Header: `This Week In OS AI Field Notes: 20 November 2026` (day without a leading zero, full month name).

Sections, in this order and with these exact headings:

1. Open-Source Models
2. Tools & Frameworks
3. Policy & Security
4. Podcasts & Media
5. What's new at Sentient?

- Within each section, order items from oldest to newest by their date. Most past sections do.
- A typical issue has 13–16 items: 3–5 in each news section, 1–3 podcasts and 1–3 Sentient items. Leave out a section that has nothing that week.

## Subject line

Base it on the issue's strongest story, use only facts the issue's items back up, and keep it to 4–12 words. Past subjects run 29–78 characters, with a median of 54. Past subjects follow three patterns:

- **The week's big theme for open-source AI**, in title case: "Open-Source AI Closes In on the Frontier Labs", "The White House Wants Control, Open-Source AI Ships Anyway", "Money Moves To Open Source AI".
- **One striking number:** "Open-Source AI Is Just 2 Months Away From the Frontier", "An open source model that cost $0.13 per task and is 45x cheaper than Opus 5?!"
- **The biggest story as a question:** "What happens to open source after Jensen?"

Offer three options, one per pattern, so the editor can pick.

## Preview text

The preview text is the line that shows next to the subject in the inbox. Older issues used "Brought to you by Sentient Foundation" every time. The latest issue lists its three lead stories instead: "Nvidia’s CEO exit, a diffusion LLM, and an EU frontier model". Write that kind: three stories in a short list, sentence case, under about 90 characters, adding to the subject rather than repeating it.
